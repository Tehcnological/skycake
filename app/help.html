How to use SkyCake

SkyCake raises the airspace and instrument procedures on FAA charts into 3D, over real terrain. It is for learning, not for navigation.

Pick an area

The start screen lists the areas, each with the date its data is current through. Open area shows its map. Save keeps a copy on this device, so the area opens without a connection; when new FAA data comes out, the button reads Update. Close, on the map, goes back to the list.

Move the map

With a mouse: drag to turn the view, right drag to move it, and scroll to zoom.

On a phone: one finger moves the map, a pinch zooms, a twist turns it, and two fingers moved up or down together tilt it.

With a keyboard: click or tab to the map, then the arrow keys turn and tilt it, Shift with the arrows moves it, and plus and minus zoom.

Raise the airspace

Airspace, top left (on a phone, the Airspace tab), lists each kind of airspace on the chart. Switch one on to raise every area of that kind from its floor to its ceiling.

To raise a single area, tap or click it on the chart. Tap a raised area to pick it, and tap it again to lower it. With a mouse, point at any area to see its name, floor and ceiling.

Find a procedure

Search, top right (on a phone, the search button in the header or the Procedure tab), finds approaches, departures and arrivals. Type an airport or a procedure's name, or pick an airport from the list, or tap its tag on the chart, to see every procedure there.

Pick one to draw it in 3D with its fixes and altitudes, inside the airspace it flies through. Add as many as you like; each is listed under the search, with an X to remove it. An approach also shows its final course, glidepath and the distance from the final approach fix to the missed approach point.

See the airport diagram

When the airport you picked last has an FAA airport diagram, Diagram lays it over the field. On desktop it is in the buttons at the bottom right; on a phone it is a tab.

Change the view

On desktop the buttons at the bottom right each show their setting; click one to change it. On a phone the View tab holds the same settings as switches and buttons.

Tags: names and altitudes on the map, for airports, fixes, airways and airspace shelves. Turn them off for a clean chart.

Height: 1× is true scale. 4× stretches every altitude, so thin shelves are easy to tell apart.

Rules: VFR shows the VFR charts with every kind of airspace. IFR shows the IFR charts with special use airspace and the low altitude airways at their minimum altitudes.

Chart: in VFR, the terminal area chart (TAC) or the sectional (SEC); in IFR, the area chart (AREA) or the low chart (LOW). Where the first one ends, the second fills in around it. Areas with only one chart for the rules skip this setting.

Walkthroughs and practice

Lessons built on SkyCake play on the sites that use it, such as your course's pages.

A walkthrough plays like a video: each step moves the view and shows a caption. The player along the bottom plays, pauses and goes back or ahead a step. Dragging the map pauses it. A quick check waits for your answer, and is not scored.

A quiz asks one question at a time, with the rest of the map dimmed. Tap what it asks for on the chart, pick an answer, or type a number. The card then says right or wrong, gives the answer, and raises it in 3D. Practice draws questions from the FAA data without end and keeps your score.

Explore, top right in both, leaves into free roam from where you are.

Accessibility mode

Turn it on with the switch on the start screen or on the map (on desktop, under the buttons at the bottom right; on a phone, at the foot of the View tab). It raises the contrast, makes controls and tags larger, stops motion, adds a camera pad with a button for every gesture, and lists what is on the map with numbers.
